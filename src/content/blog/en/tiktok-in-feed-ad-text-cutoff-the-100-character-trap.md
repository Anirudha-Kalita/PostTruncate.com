## TL;DR

TikTok's organic posts allow 2,200 characters. TikTok in-feed ads give you **100**. That's the hard cap the ads manager enforces - not a soft display fold, a real limit. Within that 100, the fold actually hits at the first line break or around the 100-character mark, so any CTA or discount code you placed after your opening line is almost certainly invisible to every user who doesn't tap "See more." The fix is to front-load your hook, value prop, and CTA into the first 50 to 70 characters - what I call the safe zone - and use a [TikTok ad preview simulator](/en/ad-previews/tiktok-ads/) to verify the fold before you put money behind it.

Imagine this: you spend a week crafting a TikTok ad for a flash sale. The creative is sharp, the targeting is dialled in, and the spend is real - $500, $1,000, maybe more. The ad gets impressions. The video plays. But conversions are flat.

You pull the ad and re-read the description. Your hook is in the first line. Your value proposition fills the second. Your promo code - `FLASH30` - sits in the third line. The third line that nobody sees.

This scenario plays out constantly in TikTok ad accounts. Media buyers write copy as if TikTok ads behave like the organic posts they see every day. They don't. The [TikTok in-feed ad text character limit cutoff](/en/ad-previews/tiktok-ads/) is one of the most quietly expensive mistakes in performance marketing, and the platform is not going to warn you about it when you're writing the description in Ads Manager.

The promo code is already behind "See more." It was always behind "See more." You just didn't know until after you'd spent.

Here's what confuses most advertisers the first time they set up a TikTok campaign.

When you post organically on TikTok, you get a generous 2,200-character limit for your caption. That's the same ceiling Instagram gives you, and most of the time it's more space than any creator actually needs. Yes, TikTok still folds the caption at the first line break or around 100 characters on the viewer side - but you have room to breathe.

When you create a non-Spark in-feed ad through TikTok Ads Manager, the description field is a different product. The platform enforces a **100-character hard cap** at the point of entry. You literally cannot type past 100 characters. The field stops accepting input.

That 2,200-character idea you had in your head? It doesn't exist in native ads. You get 100 characters. That's your entire written message.

It's a real workaround, but it requires a different workflow entirely.

## How the truncation actually works

Even within the 100-character limit, you don't reliably get all 100 characters in front of every viewer. Here's why.

TikTok collapses the caption at **the first line break or approximately 100 characters, whichever comes first.** On the full-screen vertical video interface, the caption sits below the video's lower edge, compressed against the username, music ticker, and CTA button. There's very little room before the "... See more" label appears.

This means:

-   A single hard return in your ad description triggers the fold immediately, regardless of how few characters you've used
-   Emojis count as full characters toward the 100-character limit
-   The CTA button (Shop Now, Learn More, etc.) is separate from the description and doesn't count against the limit, but it also can't replace the persuasive copy you lost to the fold

The practical consequence is brutal: **your ad description is often only 1 to 3 visible lines for the average viewer scrolling through their feed.** If your primary CTA, price, discount code, or value proposition isn't in those lines, it doesn't exist as far as most of your audience is concerned.

## Defining your safe zone: 50 to 70 characters

When I built PostTruncate's [TikTok ad preview tool](/en/ad-previews/tiktok-ads/), I tested truncation behaviour across dozens of device sizes and orientations. What I consistently found: the first **50 to 70 characters** of the description are visible to essentially every viewer, on every device, without exception.

That window - 50 to 70 characters - is your safe zone.

Everything you want 100% of your audience to see needs to live there. Your hook. Your core value proposition. Your CTA. Ideally, all three are compressed into a single punchy opening line.

After that safe zone, you enter a conditional band between roughly 70 and 100 characters. Some viewers on larger devices or in landscape-adjacent orientations will catch this text. Most won't. Treat it as bonus real estate, not guaranteed visibility.

And then there's the cliff: anything past 100 characters, or after the first hard line break, is hidden. Period.

> "Plan for 100 characters as the effective limit, not the theoretical 2,200. Treat the description field like a headline or first line of an email subject: make it count."
> 
> -- [r/PPC community guide on TikTok ad specs](https://www.reddit.com/r/PPC/)

This is exactly right. The mental model shift is from "ad caption" to "subject line." You're not writing copy. You're writing a headline.

Given the constraints, here's how I'd approach writing TikTok ad copy from scratch:

**Layer 1 - The safe zone (0-70 characters): everything critical**

Your first line has to carry the full weight of your message. One tightly written sentence that combines hook + value + action. Examples:

-   `Get 30% off today only - FLASH30 at checkout` (44 chars)
-   `New product drop. Free shipping on all orders` (46 chars)
-   `The skincare routine that worked in 30 days` (44 chars)

All three hook immediately, communicate value, and fit well inside the 70-character safe zone. None of them needs a "See more" tap to do their job.

**Layer 2 - The conditional band (70-100 characters): reinforcement**

If your safe zone line is short enough, you have a few characters left before the 100-character wall. Use them to reinforce - add a secondary detail, an urgency signal, or a qualifier. Don't introduce the CTA here for the first time. This band is unreliable.

**Layer 3 - The CTA button: your second CTA**

The CTA button (Shop Now, Learn More, Sign Up) sits outside the description field entirely. It doesn't count against your 100 characters, and it's always visible. This is where your explicit action prompt can live - but it works best as a **reinforcement** of a CTA already stated in the safe zone, not as the first mention of it.

The mistake I see most often: media buyers rely on the CTA button as their primary action driver and use the description for storytelling. On a platform where the description is nearly invisible and the video carries most of the creative load, that's the right instinct for some campaigns - but if you have a promo code, a specific offer, or a time-sensitive element, it needs to be in the description's first 70 characters. CTA buttons can't display custom text like `FLASH30`.
